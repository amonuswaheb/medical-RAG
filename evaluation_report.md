# RAG Evaluation Report

### Q1: What blood pressure threshold should trigger starting medication for hypertension?
- **Expected Source**: WHO Hypertension Guideline / Recommendation 1: Blood pressure threshold for initiation of pharmacological treatment / Page 3
- **RAG Citations**: Page 3
- **RAG Confidence**: HIGH
- **Recommendation**: WHO recommends initiating pharmacological antihypertensive treatment for individuals with a confirmed diagnosis of hypertension and a systolic blood pressure of >=140 mmHg or a diastolic blood pressure of >=90 mmHg.

### Q2: What are the three recommended first-line drug classes for treating hypertension?
- **Expected Source**: WHO Hypertension Guideline / 3.4 Drug classes to be used as first-line agents / Page 8
- **RAG Citations**: Page 8
- **RAG Confidence**: HIGH
- **Recommendation**: For adults with hypertension requiring pharmacological treatment, WHO recommends using drugs from any of the following three classes as first-line agents: (1) thiazide and thiazide-like agents; (2) angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors (ACEis)/angiotensin-receptor blockers (ARBs); and (3) long-acting dihydropyridine calcium channel blockers (CCBs).

### Q3: What is the target blood pressure for a patient with existing cardiovascular disease?
- **Expected Source**: WHO Hypertension Guideline / 3.6 Target blood pressure / Page 9
- **RAG Citations**: Page 9
- **RAG Confidence**: HIGH
- **Recommendation**: For patients with hypertension and known cardiovascular disease (CVD), WHO recommends a target systolic blood pressure treatment goal of <130 mmHg.

### Q4: How often should a patient be followed up after starting or changing antihypertensive medication?
- **Expected Source**: WHO Hypertension Guideline / 3.7 Frequency of re-assessment / Page 9
- **RAG Citations**: Page 4
- **RAG Confidence**: HIGH
- **Recommendation**: WHO suggests a monthly follow-up after the initiation of, or a change in, antihypertensive medications until patients reach their target blood pressure.

### Q5: Can nurses or pharmacists prescribe antihypertensive treatment?
- **Expected Source**: WHO Hypertension Guideline / 3.8 Administration of treatment by nonphysician professionals / Page 10
- **RAG Citations**: Page 10
- **RAG Confidence**: HIGH
- **Recommendation**: Yes, WHO suggests that pharmacological treatment of hypertension can be provided by nonphysician professionals such as pharmacists and nurses, provided that proper training, prescribing authority, specific management protocols, and physician oversight are met.

### Q6: Which antihypertensive medications are contraindicated during pregnancy?
- **Expected Source**: WHO Hypertension Guideline / 4.3 Pregnancy and hypertension / Page 10
- **RAG Citations**: Page 11
- **RAG Confidence**: HIGH
- **Recommendation**: ACE inhibitors (ACEis), angiotensin receptor blockers (ARBs), and spironolactone are contraindicated during pregnancy due to direct adverse fetal effects.

### Q7: What is the recommended starting dose in the single-pill combination treatment algorithm?
- **Expected Source**: WHO Hypertension Guideline / 6.2 Drug- and dose-specific protocols / Page 12
- **RAG Citations**: Page 12
- **RAG Confidence**: HIGH
- **Recommendation**: In the single-pill combination treatment algorithm, treatment should be started at half-maximal doses (for example, Telmisartan 40 - Amlodipine 5 mg once a day).
